Hutton Browns 7–4 Brentwood Town

The Browns lined up with Theodore Smith in goal, Hector and Pierce behind, and Theo Mills alongside captain Raff further forward.

They looked lively from the off.

Mills opened proceedings with a Cruyff turn and drove the Browns upfield, which rather set the tone for the first half. The resulting corner nearly came to something with Hector lurking on the edge of the box.

There was plenty of endeavour early on. The boys were moving well, making sensible decisions and, importantly, containing Brentwood without looking frantic.

The opener came after Raff was fouled around halfway.

Hector played the free kick into Mills, Mills worked it across the box and Raff finished.

1–0.

A couple of minutes later Pierce and Mills combined nicely and Raff was unlucky not to add another.

The second did come from the resulting corner.

Raff delivered it, Pierce kept it alive and Hector let fly from distance.

Absolute worldie.

2–0.

Then came a slightly mad couple of minutes.

Pierce picked the ball up centrally and played it into Mills, who took possession around the edge of the box and finished across goal for 3–0.

Brentwood immediately hit back.

Their number 14 found a little space and got a snap shot away to make it 3–1, very much against the run of play.

Almost from the restart, the Browns answered.

Raff found Mills, Mills just about managed to move it on to Pierce, and Pierce slipped it back across for Raff to tap in.

4–1.

Then came one of those goals you don’t really draw up on the tactics board.

Hector defended well, had to win the ball twice, then launched a huge clearance upfield. The keeper struggled to deal with it and Mills cleaned up the scraps for 5–1.

By now Jude was on and showing his usual determination to get involved in everything.

Brentwood got one back after a mix-up at the rear. Smith made the initial save well, but somehow the boys then conspired to help the ball into their own net.

5–2.

The Browns restored the cushion almost immediately.

Mills picked the ball up in midfield and drove towards goal. Whether what happened next was a pass or a slight miscontrol is probably best left to Theo himself, but Raff reacted quicker than anyone and rifled the ball into the bottom-left corner.

Hat-trick.

6–2.

The gaffer had suggested beforehand that, if the Browns ever got Brentwood on a pitch that didn’t resemble the aftermath of a good shelling, things might look a little different.

So far, point proven.

Half Time: Hutton Browns 6–2 Brentwood Town

Raff went into goal for the second half, with Mills sitting out for a spell.

The Browns started brightly again. Hector pushed higher to help play out from the back, while Jude and Smith linked nicely.

Then the game rather drifted.

Brentwood had an extra player and the Browns struggled to find the same rhythm they had in the first half. It wasn’t that Brentwood were dominating — more that Hutton seemed to lose a little of the spark they’d shown before the break.

Jude nearly provided it with a long-range effort, while Pierce became visibly livelier once he’d got his gloves on and warmed up.

Some things in football remain tactical mysteries.

Brentwood then made it 6–3 from a corner that travelled through a crowd. Raff may have been unsighted, but he swung for it, missed, and the ball ended up in the net.

The Browns’ best opportunity of the half followed through Mills, but it was Smith who finally put the game beyond doubt.

He picked the ball up in midfield and simply bullied his way down the pitch. When the Brentwood centre-back came across to stop him, Smith gave him a perfectly legal hand sandwich for his trouble and then rattled the ball into the top of the net.

7–3.

Brentwood grabbed one more right at the end, getting beyond Smith and through one-on-one with Raff. He might have closed the angle a little quicker, but by then the result was safe.

7–4.

Full Time: Hutton Browns 7–4 Brentwood Town

A game of two fairly different halves.

The first was probably some of the smartest football the Browns have played for a while. Good movement, good combinations and plenty of quality in the final third.

The second was much quieter.

Maybe the boys felt the job was done. Maybe the extra Brentwood player made it harder to find space. Either way, the intensity dropped.

Still, there was plenty to like.

Raff took his chances brilliantly, Mills was involved in everything going forward, Smith contributed at both ends and Pierce quietly had a really useful game.

But Hector stood out.

A goal from distance, an assist, huge defensive work and a real influence on how the Browns played out from the back.

17.10.26 · Perry Street 4–3 Hutton Browns — League Match 6

Perry Street 4–3 Hutton Browns

Back to Perry Street, where the previous meeting had ended 6-2 to our hosts. This time the Browns lined up with Theo Mills in goal, Hector and Jude at the back and Pierce alongside Theodore Smith further forward.

For once, there was no obvious size advantage for the opposition.

What Perry Street did have was plenty of ability.

They were organised, dangerous and clearly had some very tricky players, with their number 13 showing a lovely range of skills early on.

The Browns were right in it from the start.

Perry Street had some very tricky players, but tricks only take you so far when Theodore Smith arrives with a perfectly legal shoulder and sends you several days into the future.

The opening goal came from a slightly unusual source. A Perry Street corner somehow travelled almost the length of the pitch and dropped to Smith, who needed no invitation.

One-on-one and finish.

0–1 Browns.

Unassisted, unless we’re giving Perry Street a point for the clearance.

Smith was everywhere again. Not long after the goal, another spell of pressure from him almost produced a second.

Pierce was getting increasingly involved too, some neat footwork earning the Browns a free kick. Hector smashed it against the post, and Pierce had not one but two attempts at the rebound without quite managing to force it in.

Raff replaced Pierce and the Browns began to take control, although there was always a feeling that Perry Street needed to be kept under wraps.

The second came from exactly the sort of pressure we’ve been trying to develop.

Jude and Raff hunted high together, Jude worked the ball to Raff and Raff got himself in front of the defender before finishing for 0–2.

At the other end, Mills was having a very tidy spell in goal, making some excellent saves and dealing with everything calmly.

Eventually Perry Street found a way through, although there wasn’t much anyone could have done about it.

Their number 13 picked the ball up and hit an absolute worldie.

1–2.

Mills had no chance.

Jude nearly restored the two-goal lead before the break, only to be denied by a very good save, while Hector and Smith were doing an excellent job keeping Perry Street’s big number 7 under control.

The Browns went in ahead after a really strong first half.

There was also the small matter of the second-half conditions — sun behind us, but playing into the wind.

Half Time: Perry Street 1–2 Hutton Browns

Hector took over in goal for the second half.

Mills, now released into the outfield, introduced himself to Perry Street fairly quickly with a couple of crunching tackles and then fizzed a dangerous ball across the box which Raff was unlucky not to reach.

Perry Street levelled from a free kick for 2–2.

The Browns’ response was immediate.

Hector played the ball forward from goal to Raff, who turned, looked up and decided that getting any closer was apparently unnecessary.

He let fly from distance.

Goal.

2–3 Browns.

Pierce was now back doing Pierce things — battling for everything, annoying defenders and generally making himself difficult to play against. He won another free kick which was blocked by the wall.

Perry Street continued to threaten from distance, with one effort going narrowly over, although Hector looked to have it covered.

Then came a slightly mad spell.

Hector saved.

Perry Street hit the bar.

Then the post.

Everyone briefly forgot what was happening.

At the other end Mills broke through but was denied, before Raff started firing dangerous balls across the box. Mills and Pierce were both inches away in quick succession.

The game was now properly stretched. Perry Street had spells of pressure, but every time they pushed forward the Browns looked capable of breaking the other way.

Mills had another opportunity and pushed it narrowly wide.

Eventually Perry Street found the equaliser. Their centre-back stepped forward, the ball broke wide to two attackers and they worked the opening well to make it 3–3.

The Browns weren’t settling for the draw.

They pressed high again, Jude was desperately unlucky not to score and Mills almost buried the follow-up. Hector then produced another good save from distance at the other end.

And then came the painful bit.

Late corner.

Ball bouncing around the box.

Nobody quite able to clear it.

A Perry Street boot eventually found the ball and it ended up in the net.

4–3.

Defeat snatched from the jaws of victory.

Full Time: Perry Street 4–3 Hutton Browns

A difficult one to take because the Browns had done so much right.

Compared with the previous 6–2 defeat, this was a completely different contest.

The Browns matched Perry Street physically, created plenty themselves and were in the game right until the final kick.

Smith was everywhere again, Hector and Jude dealt brilliantly with some very tricky attackers, Mills was excellent both in goal and outfield, Pierce battled for absolutely everything and Raff was a constant threat.

Two goals for Raff, including a superb long-range finish in the second half, capped another really strong performance.

The Browns were inches away from getting something out of it.

But this time Perry Street knew they’d been in one.

10.10.26 · Hutton Browns 4–4 Intersport — League Match 5

From 4–1 down to a battling Browns comeback!

A late pitch change meant a slightly delayed kick-off for the Browns and a reunion with Intersport.

There was also the small matter of the last meeting between the sides finishing 9–2 to Intersport.

So, you know. Nothing to prove.

Pierce started in goal, with Hector captaining the side again after his Man of the Match performance last week. Theo Mills and Jude occupied the midfield with Raff up front.

Intersport started exactly as expected — quickly.

The Browns managed to snuff out the first three attacks, but the pressure eventually told. Their number 2 found number 4, who finished well to give Intersport the lead.

0–1.

For the opening five minutes, the Browns struggled to get out. Mills managed something resembling an attack, but chances were few and far between and Intersport were very much in control.

Jude produced an excellent piece of defending to come across number 4 and use his body to see off the danger, while Hector did much the same when number 2 attacked again.

Eventually the Browns created something themselves.

Raff found Theo Mills, who looked like he might be through, only for Intersport’s number 7 to arrive with a superb tackle. Their centre-back was an absolute unit and, as we would discover, wasn’t planning on letting much through him all morning.

Then, very much against the run of play, the Browns were level.

Raff fired in a kick-in, Intersport failed to deal with it and the ball eventually found Mills, who did exactly what a striker should do and tucked it away.

1–1.

The equality didn’t last long.

Pierce did brilliantly to block a long-range effort from number 4, but number 2 followed in quickest and restored the Intersport lead.

Then things started getting worryingly familiar.

Number 2 picked the ball up deep, went on a mazy solo run and made it 3–1.

A minute later, an Intersport cross was turned home by number 4.

1–4.

The Browns were suddenly in a bit of disarray and, for a few minutes, this had all the hallmarks of the last meeting.

Except this Browns side doesn’t seem quite as interested in rolling over anymore.

The comeback started with Theodore Smith finding Raff. Raff was isolated up front but had enough time to turn, work himself an angle and finish brilliantly from a tight position.

2–4.

Meanwhile Hector was having one of those afternoons where seemingly everyone on the opposition had decided either to tackle him or stand on him.

Mills was then brought down and sent the resulting free kick narrowly wide.

Straight afterwards, the Browns came again.

Jude collected the loose ball from the free kick and got his shot away. Smith kept the move alive, rescued possession and worked it back to Raff on the edge of the box.

Low finish. Corner.

3–4.

From 4–1 down, the Browns were suddenly right back in it.

What a half.

Half Time: Hutton Browns 3–4 Intersport

Jude took over in goal for the second half, although the Browns again started a little slowly.

Pierce, now outfield, immediately became involved in a running battle with Intersport’s number 2. At one stage the referee had to separate them, with the slightly beleaguered Intersport player presumably wondering exactly what he’d done to upset Pierce so much.

Probably the rebound goal in the first half.

Raff delivered a dangerous corner that Smith was unlucky not to reach, before Intersport threatened from a corner of their own. Jude parried the first effort and Smith was perfectly positioned to clear the follow-up from the line.

Intersport remained the more naturally attacking side and, in fairness, probably deserved to still be ahead.

But the Browns were making them earn absolutely everything.

Their number 7 at centre-back continued to dominate anything played directly at him. He was strong, quick and seemed to appear whenever a Brown shirt got near the box.

At some point you felt the boys might realise the solution was to go around him rather than continually trying to go through him.

Mills came back on for Raff and eventually the breakthrough arrived.

Theodore Smith drove forward and got a shot away. It was blocked, but Mills reacted first to the rebound and put it away.

4–4.

The comeback was complete.

Smith was now popping up everywhere. Attack, defence, covering runs, clearances — some wonderfully uncomplicated Paul McGrath-style positioning. Just be in the right place before everyone else realises that’s where the ball is going.

Hector continued to battle bravely despite having taken more knocks than seemed reasonable for one morning, before Raff returned in his place.

Then came perhaps the biggest moment of the half.

Intersport broke through with what looked a certain winner, only for Jude to produce a magnificent save and keep the Browns level.

That proved decisive.

Full Time: Hutton Browns 4–4 Intersport

What a difference from the start of the season.

The Browns were 4–1 down against a side who beat them 9–2 last time and could easily have decided it wasn’t their day.

Instead, they dragged themselves back into it.

Intersport had some excellent players and probably played the cleaner football for long periods. Their number 7 in particular was outstanding at the back.

But the Browns battled. And battled. And battled.

The next step is still there to see. The passing work they’re doing in training isn’t instinctive yet — too often the first thought is still to beat the man rather than find the teammate.

But it’s coming.

And when that instinct to look up and find the pass catches up with the fight these boys already have, there is plenty more to come from this side.

From 9–2 to 4–4.

That’s progress.

03.10.26 · Brentwood 3–6 Hutton Browns — League Match 4

I missed the first five minutes on the phone, which apparently was enough time for the Browns to make a flying start.

By the time I caught up, Raff had already found Pierce, who needed two bites at it but eventually forced the ball home for 1–0.

Raff then rode a tackle well before getting another chance himself. Hector found Raff, and although the first effort didn’t quite go, he stayed with it and made sure at the second attempt for 2–0.

The Browns were excellent early on. Jude and Hector were reading the game brilliantly at the back and, more importantly, talking to each other. When one stepped in, the other dropped. Simple stuff, but really effective.

Pierce was being an absolute nuisance up front, much like last week, battling for everything and making sure Brentwood never really got comfortable.

Raff thought he’d added another from a tight angle, but the referee ruled it out.

Still, the Browns had made a superb start.

Quarter: Brentwood 0–2 Browns

The third came when Theo Mills picked the ball up from Hector and tried to cross — only for the cross to keep travelling and end up in the net.

Take them however they come.

3–0.

Brentwood responded when their number 14 caught the Browns during a rare lapse in concentration, dribbled through and finished well for 3–1.

The Browns nearly restored the cushion straight away. Jude, Theo and Raff combined nicely, the ball was worked across the box and Raff’s effort was eventually blocked by the keeper’s face. Probably not how he’d planned to save it, but effective nonetheless.

Hector continued to defend really well, tracking one run and using his body to guide the attacker out before Theodore Smith, during his spell in goal, made a good save to stop another break.

Brentwood then made it 3–2 with a goal very similar to their first.

Game on.

Half Time: Brentwood 2–3 Hutton Browns

The Browns started the second half well and once again it began with Hector cleaning things up at the back. A corner was recycled by him and Mills was there to finish for 4–2.

Brentwood reacted immediately. Some much better interplay opened the Browns up and they produced a very good goal to make it 4–3.

The game had turned into a proper scrap now.

Smith was getting involved at both ends after his stint in goal, while Mills was putting himself about and collecting a few bruises for his trouble. The encouraging thing was that even as the game got messy, the Browns were still trying to pass. There were several spells where three or four boys combined rather than simply trying to force their way through.

Quarter: Brentwood 3–4 Browns

Hector came off after another really strong performance, and the Browns began to find chances again.

Mills linked nicely with Pierce and was unlucky with a left-footed effort — particularly pleasing to see him willing to use it. He then made two more similar breaks, only to find the Brentwood keeper equal to both.

At the other end, Raff dealt well with several awkward, bobbling balls on a difficult pitch.

Jude nearly created another with a dangerous cross, and when it fell to a Brentwood shirt he immediately turned and sprinted all the way back to kill the counter attack. That probably summed his game up.

The closing stages were tense, but the Browns looked the more likely side.

Eventually the pressure told.

Theo battled brilliantly, managed to thread the ball through to Pierce, and Pierce finished for 5–3.

Then came the sixth. Jude delivered a corner, the keeper got something on it but could only help it into the net.

6–3.

That finally killed the contest.

Full Time: Brentwood 3–6 Hutton Browns

A really good win, but probably more valuable because it wasn’t straightforward.

The Browns had to dig in when the game became scrappy and physical. Mills certainly knew he’d been in a game by the end of it, Pierce had to fight for everything, and Smith contributed in goal, defence and attack.

At the back, Hector and Jude were excellent. The communication between them was probably the biggest positive of the day — reading each other, covering properly and knowing when to go and when to stay.

Raff handled a difficult surface well in goal, took his own goal nicely and was unlucky not to add a couple more.

Not always pretty.

But plenty of good football, plenty of battle and another game where the Browns found a way.

26.09.26 · Hutton Browns 10–4 Tilbury — League Match 3

A smaller pitch today and, for the opening few minutes at least, the game looked like it might be one of those tight, scrappy affairs.

The Browns also had to deal with captain Raff going off early with a head injury, which didn’t help the stop-start feel.

Neither side really took control at first. Jude had the best early opportunity, squeezing one just wide of the left post, before Theodore Smith decided to take matters into his own hands.

He read a cross-field pass, stepped in, powered through the middle and finished well for 1–0.

The lead barely had time to settle before Tilbury broke the other way. Their number 5 was dragged down in the box and converted the resulting penalty to make it 1–1.

Tilbury then enjoyed a decent purple patch and started to ask a few questions.

The Browns got a chance to respond when a handball gave them a penalty of their own, but the keeper produced an excellent save and pushed the effort onto the bar.

No matter.

From the resulting corner, Jude found Smith waiting and Theo buried his second for 2–1.

Smith was now on a bit of a rampage.

The Browns defended a Tilbury corner, Smith picked up the loose ball, powered down the wing, cut inside and completed his hat-trick for 3–1.

Raff was back involved not long after and went close from a Jude pass, before Theo Mills, having enjoyed a fairly quiet spell in goal, suddenly had to pull off a brilliant double save. He then dealt with the resulting corner as well.

Tilbury did get one back before the break after some confusion at the back left their number 7 one-on-one.

Half Time: Hutton Browns 3–2 Tilbury

The second half started with much more purpose from the Browns.

Smith, now looking for passes as much as goals, slipped a lovely ball through to Mills, who finished for 4–2.

Then the Browns really started to find their rhythm.

Mills drove down the wing, cut inside and squared for Raff to tap in for 5–2.

From a kick-in, Mills then played square to Smith, who calmly put away another for 6–2.

Tilbury weren’t done, though. Their excellent number 8, Ellie, put the Browns under pressure and helped create the chance for number 7 to score from range and make it 6–3.

That sparked another spell of Tilbury pressure, but the Browns defended brilliantly to a man. Bodies in the way, last-ditch tackles, very little panic.

And quietly having an excellent half was Pierce.

He seemed to be everywhere — winning second balls, breaking things up, moving it on and generally doing the sort of work that doesn’t always get noticed but makes everything else easier. Very much the holding midfielder role.

The seventh followed a familiar pattern.

Mills down the wing, ball across, Raff finishes.

7–3.

Pierce then took it upon himself to stick close to Tilbury’s best player, won the ball back and was unlucky not to get himself on the scoresheet.

He did, however, create the next goal. Pierce battled to regain possession and found Mills, who finished for 8–3.

Mills was now causing havoc every time he got down the right. Another run ended with his first shot being blocked, but he stayed alive to the rebound and squared for Raff to complete his hat-trick.

9–3.

At the other end, Hector had taken over in goal and looked completely assured. He hadn’t had a huge amount to do, but when he was called upon he responded with an outstanding save.

Tilbury then produced probably their best football of the game, putting together three passes from the back before number 7 finished well for 9–4.

Hector followed that with another excellent stop from a deflected effort.

The Browns had the final say.

Pierce once again battled to win the initial possession and fed Mills. Theo still had plenty to do, but drove on and finished to make it 10–4.

Full Time: Hutton Browns 10–4 Tilbury

A strange one in some ways.

The first half was cagey, scrappy and much closer than the final score suggests. The second was anything but.

Once the Browns started moving the ball quicker and looking for each other, Tilbury struggled to live with them.

Smith was ruthless early on, Mills was devastating down the right, Raff came back from his knock to grab a hat-trick, Pierce quietly ran huge parts of the second half and Hector and Jude looked very comfortable in defence and Hector in goal. Jude passing now with frequency and ease

Tilbury had some good players and some really nice moments of football, but once the Browns found their rhythm there was only going to be one winner.

Not perfect.

But very, very dangerous when it clicked.

19.09.26 · Hutton Browns 5–3 Hannakins Farm — League Match 2

A first meeting with the locally infamous Hannakins Farm meant the Browns were heading into something of the unknown. One thing was obvious enough from the warm-up though — another side who looked physically bigger than us.

That has stopped being particularly interesting to the Browns.

They were by far the livelier of the two sides from the start, with Theo Mills carrying on exactly where he left off last week. He was straight into the game, drawing an early foul and going close from the resulting free kick.

The opener soon followed. Jude played a lovely ball down the line to Mills, who carried it into the box and finished confidently for 1–0.

Mills clearly fancied another.

Theodore Smith drove forward and got a shot away, the keeper could only keep it out temporarily, and Mills followed up like a proper striker to make it 2–0.

Hannakins started to come into the game after that, with their number 23 looking particularly lively. One attack looked destined for goal until Hector appeared from nowhere, tracking all the way back and timing his challenge perfectly. He almost injured himself doing it, but that ability to recover, get goal-side and pick the right moment to strike is becoming something of a hallmark.

Hannakins then had their best spell of the half. A big lad started charging down the wing with some purpose, only to find Hector waiting for him. Again.

The Browns survived the purple patch and then went the other way.

Jude delivered a dangerous corner that took a deflection off the keeper and ended up in the net for 3–0. Goal Jude. No appeals committee required.

The fourth came soon after. Another Jude pass caused problems, Theodore Smith nicked in, found himself one-on-one with the keeper and, as he tends to do in those situations, didn’t disappoint.

4–0.

Hannakins did manage to get one back through their number 13, who struck a superb long-range effort into the top of the net. Pierce, who had been solid between the posts, had very little chance with it.

Then, just before half-time, the Browns restored the four-goal cushion.

Rafferty and Theodore Smith combined to intercept number 11, squeezed the ball through to the oncoming Mills, and Theo did the rest to complete his hat-trick.

Half Time: Hutton Browns 5–1 Hannakins Farm

Hannakins started the second half much better and suddenly Smith and Hector had plenty to do.

The fact they also had an extra player on the pitch for a spell may have helped.

Jude, now in goal, looked assured from the start, while captain Pierce moved outfield and immediately got stuck into the physical battle. He went close with a long-range effort as the Browns tried to regain some control.

Raff also started the half much better defensively and was soon getting complained about from the opposition touchline for being a little too robust.

Wonders never cease.

The added aggression suited him. He won the ball in midfield, drove past two defenders and was unlucky not to finish the move himself.

Hector eventually came off after putting in an unbelievable shift, with Mills returning to the action and immediately making himself a nuisance again.

The football then took a brief break while the Hannakins sideline and coaching team made their feelings known to the referee.

After a gentle reminder that the participants were, in fact, six years old, proceedings resumed.

Back on the pitch, Theodore Smith produced two huge defensive moments in quick succession — first blocking one on the line and then, barely a minute later, clearing another away from goal.

Hannakins eventually reduced the deficit when their big number 10 picked the ball up on the right and drilled an excellent finish across goal into the far corner for 5–2.

The Browns support gave the little lad a huge cheer.

As Michelle Obama once said: when they go low, we go high.

Hannakins then made it 5–3, with the extra man beginning to have a noticeable effect. They were soon back down to five and the Browns managed the closing stages much better.

Jude produced several excellent saves late on, helped by Smith doing everything he could in front of him, and the Browns saw the game out.

Full Time: Hutton Browns 5–3 Hannakins Farm

At times it was a slightly tempestuous game, largely because Hannakins seemed increasingly frustrated by the Browns’ grit and refusal to be bullied.

The first half belonged almost entirely to Hutton. The second was much more even, helped by a much-improved Hannakins performance — and, for a while at least, an improved headcount.

But this was another really encouraging Browns display.

Mills carried on his outstanding form with a hat-trick, Hector produced another heroic defensive performance, Smith was everywhere, Jude contributed at both ends, Pierce battled throughout and Raff grew into the game nicely.

Most importantly, the old Browns trait was there again.

Battle first. Football second.

And when both turn up together, they’re becoming a very difficult little team to play against.

🔴⚫️ UP THE BROWNS 🔴⚫️
12.09.26 · Baddow 6–4 Hutton Browns — League Match 1

A proper test for the Browns in their first game, facing a Baddow side playing a year up and containing a handful of academy players. Their number 11 looked particularly handy, and it didn’t take long to see they had some good technical players throughout the side.

The Browns clearly hadn’t come just to admire them though.

Straight from the kick-off Jude nicked possession and found Theo Mills, who calmly slotted home for 1–0. Exactly the settler you want against a side like this.

Baddow started to come into it after that and their number 11 was causing problems. Hector was called upon a few times to snuff things out, Theodore Smith cleared one off the line, and Raff dealt well with a long-range effort before saving the follow-up.

The boys were under it for a spell and Baddow eventually levelled from a corner, the ball travelling through the box and unfortunately finding its way in off a Brown shirt.

The second followed fairly quickly. A bit of confusion at the back, Raff probably could have gathered it, and Baddow were alert enough to poke it home for 2–1.

Importantly, the Browns didn’t disappear.

Jude found Mills again and Theo did the hard bit himself, battling his way across the face of goal before squeezing the finish through the keeper’s legs for 2–2.

The game was properly open now. Mills was causing all sorts of trouble up front and nearly put the Browns ahead again, only for the Baddow keeper to produce an excellent save.

At the other end, Baddow showed their quality on the break. A quick move down the right followed by a switch of play left Raff one-on-one and restored their lead at 3–2.

Their number 11 continued to show some lovely skill — including the odd trick good enough to fool more than just the Browns — while Pierce was battling hard to try to release Mills whenever Hutton won it back.

Raff saved another long-range effort before Baddow’s number 14 found space across the top of the box and finished well for 4–2.

Again the Browns responded. Pierce broke forward and released Mills, who lifted his finish over the keeper to make it 4–3 and keep the game very much alive going into the break.

Half Time: Baddow 4–3 Hutton Browns

The Browns started the second half well and got their reward from a Raff corner, which took a deflection on its way in to make it 4–4.

Game on.

Pierce went close again and the match became much more nip-and-tuck. Theodore Smith, now in goal, made a strong save from distance and then another excellent stop when Baddow’s number 5 went clear through.

At the other end, Raff delivered another dangerous ball which Mills was unlucky to steer just wide, before the Baddow keeper parried a Raff corner with the Browns beginning to build some pressure again.

Pierce was putting in a shift defensively too, stopping one counter and then recovering again on the follow-up. Smith produced another excellent save from Baddow’s number 11, while Hector somehow covered the length of the pitch to make a last-ditch tackle when it looked like they were through.

Baddow eventually found the fifth. It came after some good build-up play, although Raff will probably know himself that a touch rather than trying to deal with it first time might have solved the problem. Unfortunately it ended up in the wrong net for 5–4.

To his credit, he immediately tried to make amends at the other end and was unlucky not to do exactly that.

The Browns kept pushing, but Baddow finally got some breathing room late on. The first effort was stopped, but with numbers arriving they tucked away the follow-up for 6–4.

That was that.

The scoreline says defeat, but this felt like a much better Browns outing. The battling was back, they went toe-to-toe with a technically strong team a year older, and at 4–4 there was nothing between them.

Baddow had some genuinely good footballers and probably deserved the win, but the Browns created enough chances of their own to wonder what might have happened if one or two had gone the other way.

Plenty of fight, plenty of football and, by all accounts, a big step up from last week.
